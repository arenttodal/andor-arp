# Timing in a model of Max for Live's scheduler

Generated by `./build/andor-arp-timing-report` (tests/SchedulerSimulation.cpp), 20 November 2026,
andor-arp 0.1.0-dev, core 1.0.0. **A model, not a measurement in Live** (spec §7: "do not ship before
measuring its timing accuracy"; the Live measurement is M4L-T1 in `docs/TEST_MATRIX.md`).

What is compared: the same lane (UP/DOWN 1/16, 2 octaves, gate 70 %, unless noted) rendered
(a) as a plugin would, in 64-sample blocks with exact transport positions (the reference), and
(b) through `MaxSchedulingAdapter` as `andor.arp.engine` runs it: services once per scheduler
vector (64 samples), transport readings published once per Live audio buffer (stale by up to a
buffer), keys reaching the external at the next service without a timestamp, notes output at the
first service at or after their scheduled time (clock callbacks fire on ticks). Deviations are
note-on times after removing the configured output latency (4 ms); positive is late. 30 s each.

Reading the table:

- **No note is lost, doubled or left hanging** in any scenario, including loops, a tempo change,
  clock drift, 20 ms stalls and clip chord changes exactly on bar lines.
- **Typical deviation ~2 ms late** after the 4 ms latency: one scheduler vector, because a block
  covers the time since the last tick and its notes go out on the next tick. Without tick
  quantisation (last row) the algorithm itself is within 1.8 ms (mean 0.4 ms).
- **Stalls longer than the latency** make notes late by up to the stall (17 ms here); nothing is
  lost. A larger `@latency` trades responsiveness for stall tolerance.
- **Re-anchors** happen only at the transport's start and at each loop wrap.

Findings that changed the design while building this model:

1. Rendering ahead of time (an earlier version scheduled up to latency + 2 ms ahead) applied keys
   after grid lines already rendered: a release on a grid line played one extra step, and a clip's
   chord change on a bar line would have played one step of the old chord. Fixed by rendering just
   in time on Live's timeline and applying keys at the block's start.
2. Anchoring to a single stale reading at transport start left an error of up to a buffer that the
   0.5 % correction took a second to remove (worst case ~5 ms). Fixed by trusting the most advanced
   reading of the last 30 ms and allowing a 3 % correction for half a second after a re-anchor.

Open (to measure in Live): how stale ITM readings really are, `clock_fdelay` resolution in Live,
and how Live time-stamps MIDI coming out of a device inside a Rack chain.
